## BID: government bidding

**The work** (RA 12009, the New Government Procurement Act, in force 13 Aug 2024; its IRR 25 Feb 2025):

1. Plan: agencies post their Annual Procurement Plan by end-January; market scoping sets the
   budget (ABC). Government buys PNF medicines only; the DOH Drug Price Reference Index is the
   ceiling (a range since June 2026).
2. Invitation to Bid on PhilGEPS for 7 days (PhilGEPS Platinum needed).
3. Pre-bid conference when the ABC is ₱3M or more, 12+ days before the deadline; questions 10 days
   before; bid bulletins 7 days before.
4. The bid: envelope 1 (eligibility and technical: PhilGEPS certificate, ongoing contracts, a
   similar contract worth 50% of the ABC in 10 years, NFCC or credit line, bid security, Omnibus
   Sworn Statement), envelope 2 (price). Above the ABC is out. Bid security: a declaration, 2% cash
   or 5% bank guarantee/surety. Valid up to 120 days.
5. Opening (lowest price wins: both envelopes the same day).
6. Post-qualification: the lowest bidder's papers in 5 days, checked in 12 (45 at most).
7. Notice of Award within 10 days; performance security (5% cash or bank guarantee, 30% surety)
   and the contract within 10 days of it; opening to award 60 days at most.
8. Notice to Proceed, then delivery: 0.1% a day for late delivery, cancellable at 10%.
9. Inspection and Acceptance Report, then payment: 1% retention for 3 months on medicines;
   government withholds 5% VAT and 1% income tax (BIR 2307). Missing papers can hold payment for
   years.

Other roads: small value procurement up to ₱2M (one quote enough), direct acquisition up to ₱200k,
direct contracting (exclusive distributor), negotiated after two failed biddings or for life-saving
medicines, repeat orders (25% within 6 months), framework agreements (call-offs over 1–3 years).

**The bid report** (Aryan: "where they went to bid, how much they quoted, was ours approved or not,
why not, how low the price went"): each tender with the agency and its place, the date, the ABC,
our price, every bidder's price and rank, the lowest, won / lost / failed, the reason (above the
ABC, documents, post-disqualified, failed bidding), how far we were from the winner.

**The head sees:** bids joined, won and lost (count and ₱), the win rate by agency and medicine,
the loss reasons, awarded against target, money owed by agency and age (net of withholding),
securities still out (bid, performance, retention) with their release dates.
**The manager (Bidding Admin) sees:** new notices for our medicines, each bid's calendar (pre-bid,
questions, opening, the 5-day papers, the 10-day contract and security, delivery), late-delivery
penalties building, company papers ending, deliveries waiting for the IAR, invoices not yet
stamped received.
**The coachee sees:** their own bids and their calendar, the hospitals they serve, what each owes.

## CLIDP

The FDA's **Certificate of Listing of Identical Drug Product**: a distributor sells our medicine
(same maker, same formula) under its own brand (sugammadex as Aemadex, Godagamet, Sugamacen). It
needs a notarized distributorship agreement with 2MG (the CPR holder), both LTOs and the labels;
₱3,000 a year branded (+₱500 brand clearance) or ₱2,000 unbranded; 30 working days to process;
it lives as long as our CPR. From Zoho: 173 customers, bulk orders (median 50 units), mostly Net
30, about 153 days to collect, a minimum order on each price line.

**The CLIDP page** (Aryan: "how many pharmacies we went to, did they approve us, at what rate"):
each distributor or pharmacy approached, with the stage (approached → interested → agreed → listing
filed → listed → ordering), the rate agreed per medicine, the brand and certificate with their
validity, the agreement's expiry, the volume and money owed.
**Subir sees:** approached, agreed and listed counts this month and in all; volume against target;
listings and agreements ending in 60 days; money owed by distributor.
**The Sales Coaches see:** their accounts by stage, who to visit or call next, reorders due against
each account's own gap, listings to renew.

## B2B: CRR and NBD

NBD brings new accounts: lead → LTO checked on the FDA portal (buying from an unlicensed seller is a
critical deficiency for a drugstore) → papers → first order → second order (activated) → handed to
CRR at the 3rd order or day 90. CRR keeps them buying: each account against its own reorder gap,
called before it is due; an account that stops is flagged and goes back to NBD. Lead source: the
drugstores PhilHealth accredits for GAMOT.

Sides (Aryan, 19 Nov, "decide later"): CRR Fritzie Mier and Claire Gallana (Mindanao); NBD
Floremel, Lonie, Winbelle, Benjie and the four new seats (Dhon, Pillow, Charle, Mani Mishra).

**The head sees:** active accounts (ordered within 90 days), opened and activated this month,
lost, revenue from CRR against NBD, money owed by age, where they buy (by city).
**Jessa (manager) sees:** CRR accounts due or late, stopped; NBD leads called, papers missing,
first and second orders; calls and orders per person.

## HOSP: hospitals

Accreditation (SEC/DTI, BIR 2303, mayor's permit, FDA LTO, CPRs) → formulary (a doctor asks, the
Pharmacy & Therapeutics Committee approves; government hospitals buy PNF only) → price or contract
(government hospitals by bidding) → PO or consignment → delivery → invoice → collection (PhilHealth
pays the government hospitals late).

**Vanessa sees:** each team (Julius, Daniel, Claire, Ken, Jim Lord) against target, hospitals by
sales, money owed by age, formulary and accreditation dates.
**A team lead sees:** each CHC's day (who needs them, sales on pace, days to collect, lapsed
hospitals, the evening huddle), the hospitals of the team with who is due or late.
**A CHC sees:** their hospitals, whom to call, money to collect, their target.

## MT: modern trade

Accreditation → new-item listing → yearly trading terms (listing fees, shelf, rebates) → the
chain's POs (DC or stores) → delivery and fill rate → sell-out data → bad orders and near-expiry
returns → payment net of deductions.
**Mhalou sees:** sell-in by chain against target, listings and stores, deductions open, money owed
and days to collect by chain, returns; her two sellers' accounts.

## RX Business and MD Telesales

Rx CHCs keep a doctor list by class (A three visits a month, B two, C one), call plan, visits,
samples; doctors prescribe; patients buy with the Rx on file. Guarantee letters (PCSO, DSWD,
Malasakit, the Office of the President): quotation → GL → serve → bill the agency → paid.
MD Telesales work the call list: Rx check, quote, order, delivery, refill.
**Javed sees:** sales by line and person against target, doctor coverage, new prescribers, GLs by
agency from issued to paid, patients active and dropped.
**Honey sees:** per person calls against plan, coverage, class A doctors missed, refills due,
referrals not yet called, GLs ending in 7 days.

## TeleSales (anesthesia, hospitals)

The call list (callbacks, reorders, quotes, RFQs), orders, deliveries, collections. Controlled
anesthesia medicines need the buyer's PDEA licence and a Local Order Permit per transfer.
**Reynan sees:** per person calls, orders and sales against target, reorders due, money owed,
orders blocked for a licence or permit.
